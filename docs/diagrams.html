1 · What "insert a clip" actually means

The main video is cut at keyframes and clips are laid in between the cuts, so the player's currentTime sees one continuous timeline. Blocks are drawn to scale — a 9 s clip really is a sliver next to a 30 minute source.

Nothing after the first cut keeps its old timestamp. Every virtual time downstream shifts by the length of the clips before it — which is exactly why an edit invalidates the buffer but never the element.

2 · Pipeline · two lanes, one invariant

Video is 95 % of the bytes and is never copied — mp4box hands back valid fMP4 fragments that MSE accepts directly. Audio is tiny, so it is sliced at exact sample boundaries and re-muxed. That asymmetry is what keeps A/V locked.

3 · Buffer window & eviction

A 30-minute source cannot stay buffered — Chrome throws QuotaExceededError long before that. The scheduler keeps a sliding window and drops everything behind the playhead.

4 · Audio modes

Main audio cannot simply keep running under a clip: it advances 9.4 s while the main video holds at the cut, so afterwards they sit 9.4 s apart — permanently, and the error compounds with every insertion. These three modes are what is physically coherent.

5 · Four engines, one edit model

All four consume the same Timeline. Switching engines re-appends fragments — it never re-indexes media and never loses the edit.
